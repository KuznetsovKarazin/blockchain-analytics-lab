The experiment estimates notification coverage for transactions associated with fixed historical protocol components under specified observation, initialization and notification-budget rules. The nominated components are the Li.Fi user entrypoint and the Revest entrypoint and custody vault. Their addresses remain fixed throughout each window. This estimand does not encompass every protocol-owned contract, every asset holder, or all historically active routing. Component nomination used incident-informed historical documents. The cases are external to the previously frozen detector methods, but neither nomination nor the investigation was globally blind to the incidents.

Li.Fi and Revest constitute a source-limited cohort selected after bounded dispositions of a 13-row catalogue. They represent two verified incident families, not a random or representative sample of incidents, protocols or chains. Unresolved and source-unavailable catalogue entries retain their dispositions rather than becoming negative outcomes. Each intended case contributes a fixed 30-day UTC window ending after its incident date: 60 case-days spanning 37 distinct calendar days. Overlapping dates, transactions, tokens and method–policy cells do not create additional independent incidents. The protocol therefore specifies descriptive comparisons without a population-level significance test.

All methods receive the same selected transactions and audited observations. Selection requires at least one log emitted by a nominated core address, without filtering by topic, score or reference label; each selected transaction requires its complete all-emitter log bundle and successful execution metadata. The primary comparison is the frozen token-outflow rank rule at threshold 1/20 against the ten-core-log, 60-second count rule, at an offered daily budget of five notifications. Greedy, paced-cumulative and token-bucket policies are reported separately. Threshold 1/10 and budgets one and twenty are prespecified sensitivities. Feature history, grouping and budgets reset independently at every UTC-day boundary. This initialization is part of the estimand, rather than a reconstruction of continuous historical monitor state. Equal offered budgets need not produce equal realized notification loads.

Coverage is decomposed from independently verified reference executions through available observation evidence, core-emitter selection, positive core-sender outflow, sufficient token history, raw trigger, grouped candidate and emitted notification. The count arm bypasses outflow and history eligibility. The semantic arm requires at least 19 strictly earlier positive observations for a token within the current day; absent and zero outflows do not populate that history. Cold start therefore applies to token histories, not simply the first 19 transactions. Grouping and budget suppression remain separate outcomes. An execution receives credit only when that exact transaction emits a notification; a nearby or grouped transaction cannot substitute for it.

These rules imply deterministic coverage limits before measurement. Let \(P_d\), \(S_d\) and \(N_{m,d}\) denote reference, selected and notified transactions on day \(d\). For a nonempty reference set \(P\), \(N_{m,d}\subseteq S_d\) and \(|N_{m,d}|\le B\) imply that fully observed execution coverage is bounded above by \(\sum_d\min(B,|P_d\cap S_d|)/|P|\). Replacing \(S_d\) with semantic-eligible transactions gives a potentially tighter bound. These are consequences of selection and capacity, not empirical findings or a new detection theorem. Unselected, no-outflow and cold-start references remain in the outer denominator.

Both cases' decisions must be sealed before new reference retrieval and exact-identity joining. Reference inventories default to known subsets unless positive closure evidence establishes completeness. With missing observations, coverage over the known reference set is bounded by \(n/|P|\) and \((n+u)/|P|\), where \(n\) is notified and \(u\) unresolved. These bounds do not cover undiscovered executions. A verified notification establishes an incident hit; absence of a hit establishes a complete miss only with complete execution inventory and observations. Otherwise incident coverage remains indeterminate. Unadjudicated notifications are unknown, precluding precision or false-positive-rate estimates.

The observation boundary remains enumerated archive partitions and checked transaction metadata, without authenticated receipt-root or universal chain-completeness claims. ERC20-shaped events and raw token-separated amounts do not establish token compliance, balance changes or economic loss. Missing or malformed required evidence halts comparison rather than shortening windows or imputing empty data. Block timestamps cannot establish live detection latency, prevention or intra-transaction causality. Any contribution assessment depends on completed independent audits and the resulting coverage–load comparisons; implementation, provenance recovery and incident reconstruction alone do not establish detector superiority.
